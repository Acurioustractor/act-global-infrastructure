# PICC On Country Photo Studio

> A portrait studio that travels to Palm Island, run on community terms. The photographs stay with the people in them.

**Status:** Active | **Code:** `ACT-PS` | **Parent:** [[picc|PICC (Palm Island Community Company)]]

## What it is

A mobile portrait and documentary studio for Bwgcolman (Palm Island). It sets up where people already are, community events, the PICC precinct, family gatherings, and makes formal photographs the way a town studio once did: lit, considered, printed, handed over.

It sits beside the [[picc-photo-kiosk|PICC Photo Kiosk]]. The kiosk is the everyday archive, the snapshot families reach for. The studio is the deliberate portrait. Two tiers of one visual infrastructure, both under community control.

## The archive so far

The first studio sittings ran 26 to 28 September 2025. Those three days produced 501 photographs, now held in [[empathy-ledger|Empathy Ledger]] against this project. All 501 are private. Eleven carry recorded consent. Nothing from the studio is published anywhere yet, and nothing should be until the people in the frame have said yes through the Empathy Ledger consent flow.

Those figures were read from the Empathy Ledger database on 6 September 2026 and will move. The live count lives in Empathy Ledger, not on this page.

## Why a studio

Photographs of Palm Island have mostly been taken by people passing through, for uses the community did not choose. The studio inverts the geometry. Community members sit for a portrait because they want one. The file belongs to them. Consent is a decision they make per image, revocable, recorded. The same [[../concepts/ocap-principles|OCAP principles]] that govern stories in Empathy Ledger govern the pictures.

## Where it goes

The studio is an art project as much as a service. The portraits are the material for exhibitions, books and the Palm Island knowledge base, and each of those uses waits on consent. When a set is cleared, it can be syndicated through Empathy Ledger to PICC's own surfaces and, where the storyteller agrees, to the ACT studio site and JusticeHub.
