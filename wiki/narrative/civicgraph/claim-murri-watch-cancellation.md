## The argument

Murri Watch is not a boutique program. It is the service that has sat with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander kids inside Queensland watch-houses since 1991 — the year the Royal Commission handed down its findings. The average watch-house stay for a child in Queensland is 161 hours, and some stays run past 14 days (UNVERIFIED: no source recorded; check before use). For 35 years, Murri Watch was the service inside those hours.

The end of funding is not a funding cut dressed as a transition. It is a funding cut framed as a transition. See the companion claim `claim-gerber-22-unnamed.md` for the minister's counter-narrative and why the transition language collapses without a public list.

## Expanded context

- Uncle Adrian Coolwell, 30 years QPS and the state's first assistant watch-house officer, said publicly on 14 April that the cancellation puts "a lot of weight" back on general duties police.
- The cancellation announcement coincided with a separate government claim that $560M is going to 22 First Nations-led organisations delivering 28 new services (see `claim-gerber-22-unnamed.md`).
- Adam Robinson at StreetSmart Australia has been funding Murri Watch with small grants since 2018 because larger funding never came. He publicly called the decision terrible on 14 April.
- Bernice Hookey (Cultural Strategist, Founder MZB Empowerment) publicly wrote that Murri Watch gave her peace of mind when her own family member was in the watch-house.
