# A film about the young people who walked out the other side

*Draft brief for a First Nations director and producer. JusticeHub, A Curious Tractor. September 2026. Not for circulation until Ben has read it aloud.*

## The question we're bringing you

We want to film the places working so young people never see the inside of a cell.

In Mparntwe, Kristy Bloomfield and Tanya Turner of Oonchiumpa have brought 55 judges from across the country onto Country to learn what happens after a sentence is handed down. That is one community. JusticeHub works alongside others doing the same kind of work on their own terms, and a film could follow them over three years: the young people, the elders, the workers, the magistrates who came to listen, the days it fails and the days it holds.

We can't make that film. We don't have the credits and we shouldn't hold the camera. We're looking for the people who should.

## What exists

JusticeHub is a youth justice evidence and storytelling platform run by A Curious Tractor. Its three-year program plan has a feature documentary at the centre, co-directed with each community, with story sovereignty held by the community throughout. The program is not yet funded. The film today is an idea with relationships behind it.

Around it: CONTAINED, the touring shipping-container installation about youth detention, and The Brave Ones, a portrait series in development that would give young people the dignity the media never has, with their consent held through Empathy Ledger.

## Why you, and why now

Screen Australia's First Nations Documentary Development fund offers up to $30,000 for a feature or series documentary intended for a broadcaster or platform, and up to $20,000 for a one-hour television or online series. The next round closes **22 October 2026**, then 14 January, 11 March and 6 May 2027. The applicant must be an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander writer, producer or director, and so must the director and any co-directors. Writers and directors need an eligible documentary credit; a producer needs to be experienced, hold two credits, or work alongside someone who does. Those rules put the film where it belongs.

## What we'd bring

Introductions to the communities, and their say at every step, including the right to say no. The evidence base JusticeHub holds on what works in youth justice. Research and logistics. A Curious Tractor or The Butterfly Movement as a co-financing partner, not the applicant. Anything we can find in the way of development money beyond Screen Australia.

## What we'd ask

A conversation first. If it fits, you lead the application and the creative, we hold the door open.

## Still open

- **Title.** Our own papers carry two, *The Country We're Building* and *The Brave Ones*. The director and communities should choose.
- **Which communities.** The conversations have started in Mparntwe (Alice Springs), Minjerribah (Stradbroke Island), the Utopia homelands and more. Each community decides for itself whether a camera comes, and on what terms. That comes before anything else.
- **Round.** 22 October is five weeks away. January is the realistic round if the first conversations take time, and they should.
